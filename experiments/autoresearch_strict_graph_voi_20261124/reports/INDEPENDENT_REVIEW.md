# Independent ACL-style review

## Summary

The paper presents a graph-conditional certificate revalidation controller for
reusing communication transcripts after graph edits. The strongest evidence is
the rows2 confirmatory service panel and the semantic rows4 extension.

## Strengths

- The research question is concrete and operationally important.
- The strict certificate has a short proof, a tight equality counterexample and
  a no-drift degeneration.
- Baselines share old transcripts, changed rows, model checkpoints, stopping
  targets and token ledgers.
- Two tasks and two checkpoints provide an independent service panel.
- Ranking ablations show that mass-aware ordering is cheaper than random,
  periodic and relay-distance ordering under the same certificate.
- Exact-cache failures are retained, so the paper does not claim universal
  dominance.

## Major risks

- Decision-region certificates and cost-aware test ordering have close prior
  art. Novelty must stay on graph-conditioned transcript validity and dirty-cone
  transport.
- The CPU H2 gate has a topology boundary. Diamond and mixed graphs improve by
  7.00% and 6.41%, below the aggregate 10% threshold.
- The real service panel is small and uses fixed interface fixtures. The result
  does not establish production latency or broad task accuracy gains.
- The local PDF build is blocked by the sandbox TeX environment. A submission
  package still needs an external ACL-compatible compile and visual audit.

## Verdict

The evidence supports a focused mechanism paper at ACL-level scope if the
claims remain risk-constrained and the topology boundary is explicit. It does
not support a universal graph-optimization or answer-quality-improvement claim.
The most important final check is an external clean checkout and PDF compile.
