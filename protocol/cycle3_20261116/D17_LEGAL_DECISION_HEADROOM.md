Exploratory development diagnostic, frozen before execution; zero new inference. Uses all D16 outcomes only after complete execution/input/score audits. D15/D16 credit-correlation gates remain unchanged. This asks a different question: can refreshed feedback improve a LEGAL edit decision, even if individual credit correlations fail? No learned predictor or policy efficacy claim.

Use the existing two tasks, all96 questions, all2 source realizations and4 terminal seeds. Keep the SAME D15 label-free shock h. Current graph B=7 xor(1<<h); the shocked edge cannot be restored in this diagnostic. Allowed primary actions: keep B, or delete exactly one of its two remaining edges. All source generation included in the chosen graph is charged, including filtered notes. Utility remains EM-5e-6*tokens. Terminal halves are[0,1] and[2,3], averaging both source realizations. Transfer both directions without selecting a favorable half.

Stale-feedback oracle: on the selection half, compare full-graph utility U(7) and U(7-e) for the two currently remaining edges e; select the largest deletion gain, or keep if all gains<=0. Apply that action to B on the other half. Refreshed-feedback oracle: select the best legal current action on the selection half, evaluate on the other half. Tie order is keep, then deletion in increasing edge index. These use selection-half gold and are optimistic diagnostics, not deployable current-question feedback policies.

Strong fixed comparator: four policies (always keep; always delete edge e if available, otherwise keep, e=0,1,2). Report all. Compare with retrospectively best policy ON THE EVALUATION HALF; this is a deliberately strong oracle comparator. Also report cross-question two-fold selection of a fixed policy, using deterministic parity of archived index; question folds never split seeds. Report paired utility/EM differences, corrections and destructions per source/terminal combination, legal-action counts and choice distributions. Descriptively also report an oracle over all legal submasks of B, including empty graph; this cannot substitute for the primary single-edit test.

No correlation requirement in THIS NEW diagnostic. No CI/bootstrap; no independent confirmation; no formal round. Failure freezes the current short full-context star single-edit feedback route. Passing permits a new implementable acquisition-policy contract with separate feedback and service questions, all acquisition/repetition costs, static/random/periodic/uncertainty controls and genuinely fresh confirmation. It does not rescue D14/D15/D16, and does not count as evidence of a novel method.

Advantages: tests the research decision target directly, respects the exogenous graph shock, and costs no new GPU time. Risks: selection-half oracle labels are unavailable to a real service policy, repeated source averaging is not free deployment, small exposed development sets and a three-edge star limit generality.
