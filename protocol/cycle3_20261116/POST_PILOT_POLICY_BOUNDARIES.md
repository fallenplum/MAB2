# Policy-stage boundaries fixed during unscored pilot collection

The evidence-indexed pilot is a system-admission experiment. Even a passing pilot is not an effective credit-maintenance method. No new policy result is inferred from engineering or exact-cache equality.

A key cost counterexample: when the terminal prompt already canonicalizes the union of documents, deleting a redundant incoming edge may leave both reward and token cost unchanged. If the sender prefix was already generated to establish redundancy, its cost must still be charged. Terminal-ancestor elimination cannot retroactively erase that observed prefix. Therefore do not translate certificate coverage directly into saved service cost.

Any subsequent mechanism comparison must share canonical union, exact request reuse and dead-node elimination with all baselines. Free equality certificates and free protect-changed/coverage policies must be available to baselines as well. Count all source selectors, relay selectors, terminal answers, initial labels, acquired counterfactuals, verification, transfer overhead and failed physical calls. Logical token utility and actual wall/GPU cost remain separate ledgers.

If pilot gates pass, use its outputs only as development data to diagnose whether noncertified graph-induced evidence changes predict harmful credit reuse. A formal adaptive policy then needs a newly frozen contract and fresh question groups. Candidate hypotheses include allocating counterfactual checks to changed exclusive evidence and decision-near-threshold edges; each must beat random/periodic/uncertainty, free structural rules, stale credit and fixed graph/strong single-model controls at equal complete budget. No specific hypothesis is declared selected before pilot analysis. If the pilot fails, retain it and move to outer-loop analysis; do not pick another graph from its matrix as a confirmatory winner.

Scientific risks: exact certificates are established cache equivalence; coarse document selectors can lose information; aggregate credit can be too sparse for feedback to repay its cost. These are plausible explanations to falsify, not reasons to add an unvalidated complex predictor.
