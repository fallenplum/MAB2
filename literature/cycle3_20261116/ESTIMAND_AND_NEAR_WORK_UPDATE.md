# Credit estimands and the nearest practical limitation

Primary-source recheck:2026-11-16. This is a scoped update, not an exhaustive survey or author-code reproduction.

C3 substitutes sampled messages at a recorded prefix and executes downstream continuations; its unbiased-advantage result requires stated restoration and sampling assumptions. Our edge-removal contrast is a different estimand. A changed downstream observation law is the intended edge intervention, not automatically estimation bias. We cannot import its training-gradient theorem or call two paired seeds an exact expected credit. [C3 v4, Sections2.2–2.4,6 and AppendixB.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06859v4).

Filtered-feedback work allocates exact evaluations for a gradient-weighted estimation-risk objective, with independence assumptions and unknown error quantities. That optimality does not imply optimal graph-edit decisions under our complete service-and-feedback budget. [Ben-Gal and Tong v2, Section7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22785v2).

Wrong but Useful v2 distinguishes fixed-pool receiver benefit from proposal correctness. Its practical learned selector does not establish improvement on new problems, and its same-problem replay selection uses reference answers and additional calls. Thus repeatable counterfactual effects alone are insufficient evidence for deployable acquisition. The v2 record adds Feng Yan; retain explicit version provenance rather than copying the older cited author list. [Yang et al. v2, Sections3,4.5 and AppendicesE,H](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14375v2).

For MAB2 define $c_e(x,G)=E[R(x,G)]-E[R(x,G-e)]$, and the net deletion gain $d_e(x,G)=-c_e(x,G)+\lambda E[C(x,G)-C(x,G-e)]$. Changing $G$ changes both terms. A useful recheck must change a downstream action enough to pay its acquisition cost over its declared service horizon. This is our decision objective, not a claim that the formula is novel. Prerequisite observations remain paid even if a final chosen graph would otherwise skip their nodes.
